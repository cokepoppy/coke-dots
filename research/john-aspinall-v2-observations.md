## Replication implications

1. Keep the first-run computer choice before the welcome conversation. The first card is selected by default; the local-computer row has its own enabled switch. The screen says this choice can change later. The exact first-card detail is too blurred to transcribe; map the option only to a capability we can verify. Coke Dots stores the choice by tenant and exposes it again in profile settings. Its local service uses a tenant-isolated Chrome profile and does not imply macOS file or app access.
2. After the 01:45 setup transition, show the observed transient “The computer is connected to your dot” status while entering the welcome conversation. The five-second dismissal is a Coke Dots implementation choice; the video only bounds it between sampled frames. Preserve the separation between the dot conversation, its details/activity side panel, and the distinct computer viewport. In observed computer states, status text identifies who currently controls the computer and a “Take over” action is visible.
3. The Color/Characters/Pets editor and the Shape/Eyes/Glasses/Accessories editor are both directly visible. Chrome samples near 02:39 disagree at one-second granularity, and the 02:41 first-editor note has no retained frame; the recording does not establish a reliable transition or whether these are separate entry points. Do not interpret them as ordered setup stages until a clear click-through is observed. The local build's visible Dot identity control remains an implementation choice pending direct verification. Tiny option names, pet behavior, and fresh-session persistence are unverified.
4. The 01:57 recheck still shows the computer-choice screen. In the 04:02–04:07 Chrome recheck, the named avatar is saved before the naming prompt, customization pill, and suggestion card appear; “Roger it is! ❤️” is visible by 04:12, but the frame does not establish its speaker. The suggestion-card body is unreadable, and no frame shows the Dot scheduling or executing those suggestions.
5. Represent task proposals, reported in-progress work, completed outputs, and user-visible notifications as different states. This video shows all four concepts being discussed or displayed, but it also includes a reported missed notification and does not independently establish a schedule run.
6. The 07:12 detail panel exposes a Slack action. Separate Chrome samples at 07:14 and 07:21 show a workspace-selection modal; they do not show OAuth consent or a successful connection. Coke Dots implements the visible picker and tenant-scoped OAuth setup, but Slack message delivery remains unimplemented. See [Slack contact setup evidence](slack-contact-setup.md).
7. At 12:50 and 12:55, the source computer viewport shows Chromium's native blocked-page state for `www.amazon.com`. Use this only as a negative browser-state reference: Amazon is not a Dots feature, and the footage does not establish why Chromium blocked the request or what Reload does. The local E2E fixture reproduces the observed browser-native error state in test mode; it does not add Amazon-specific policy or integration to the product.
8.
